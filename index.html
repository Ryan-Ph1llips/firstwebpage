What Is Deforestation?

Forests are one of the most important parts of our planet. They provide homes for millions of animal and plant species, absorb carbon dioxide and help keep our environment healthy. However, forests around the world are being destroyed at an alarming rate.

Deforestation is the large-scale removal of trees to make room for farming, buildings, roads and other human activities. When forests disappear, wildlife loses its habitat and ecosystems become increasingly threatened.

Why Are Forests Important?

Wildlife Habitats

Forests provide food, shelter and breeding grounds for countless animal species. When forests are destroyed, animals can lose their homes and face the threat of extinction.

Learn More

Climate Protection

Trees absorb carbon dioxide and store carbon, helping reduce greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Destroying forests releases stored carbon and contributes to climate change.

Learn More

Clean Water

Forests help protect water sources, reduce soil erosion and maintain the water cycle. Without trees, soil can wash into rivers and water supplies can become damaged.
